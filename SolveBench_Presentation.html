01 · Base Paper

What It Studies

P. Khrapov & N. Volkov (2023). Comparative Analysis of Jacobi and Gauss–Seidel Iterative Methods. arXiv:2307.09809 [math.NA] Domain — Numerical Linear Algebra, Iterative Methods for SLAEs (Syllabus Weeks 5–6)
  • Derives the exact convergence range of the Jacobi and Gauss–Seidel methods by tracking where the roots of a characteristic polynomial — a Hurwitz-style stability criterion — fall inside the unit circle.
  • Works this out fully for small SLAEs — 2 and 3 unknowns — with complex and real coefficients, then backs the theory with a statistical Python comparison of convergence for matrices from 2 to 5 unknowns.
  • Shows the two methods do not always share the same convergence region — which method wins depends on the structure of the matrix.
The gap this leaves open The analysis stops at toy systems of 5 unknowns or fewer, using only two solvers. Real engineering systems have thousands of unknowns — nobody in the paper tests these convergence claims on real data.

02 · Problem & Scope

Problem Statement & Scope

Every simulation that models a power grid or checks a structure's safety must, at its core, pick a solver for A⃗x = b⃗. Pick wrong, and a simulation that should take milliseconds either crawls — or fails to converge at all.
